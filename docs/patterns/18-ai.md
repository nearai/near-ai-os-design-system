# AI and agent patterns

NEAR AI builds private, verifiable AI, so its interfaces carry a stricter duty than most AI products. Every statement about where data went is backed by something a person can inspect, and every action an agent takes is visible, approvable and, where possible, reversible. This section covers how people start AI work, watch it happen, judge the result, approve what agents do, and decide what leaves their device. It builds on the `Message`, `PromptComposer`, `ProofTag`, `AgentRun` and `ToolCall` components, and on `ConsentBlock`, `CodeBlock`, `Toast` and `Notice`.

## Principles

1. **Say where AI is used.** Generated content is labelled as generated, and nobody is led to believe a person wrote it.
2. **Proof rather than promises.** Copy states data consequences and shows proof states. It never claims that something is private, secure or protected.
3. **Nothing leaves the device without consent.** People see exactly what would be sent before it is sent. "Nothing is sent unless you say so."
4. **People stay in charge.** Every result can be copied, edited, retried, adjusted or undone. Agents ask before acting, and never perform destructive actions or purchases on their own.
5. **Specific words while waiting.** Progress text says what is happening ("Reading 3 files"), never a vague "Thinking…".
6. **Never steal focus.** Streaming output, tool calls and finished runs never move keyboard focus or yank the scroll position.
7. **Unknown is unknown.** A proof state, count or status that has not been reported reads as unknown. It is never rounded up to the nearest good answer.

## Disclosure of AI use

- Name the author of every `Message`. The author line carries the agent or model name supplied by the product ("NEAR AI agent", or the model's name), in `<tier>-label` with `text-secondary`. Never give an agent a human name, a human photo or a typing style that imitates a person.
- Mark generated text that leaves the thread. When a result is inserted into a document, a reply or a form, keep a caption such as "Drafted by NEAR AI agent" beside it until the person edits or accepts it.
- Show where each answer was produced with a `ProofTag` that names the destination (see *Route disclosure*).
- Set expectations before first use. Explain in two sentences what the feature can and cannot do, and place one plain limit line near the composer on first use and in an empty thread ("Answers can be wrong. Check facts that matter."). Do not repeat it on every message.
- Keep a non-AI path wherever AI is complementary. If the model is unavailable or switched off, the person can still finish the task by hand, and the unavailable state names that path.
- Keep system chrome, errors and notices impersonal; only the agent's own turns may say "I". See *Writing and content*.

## Prompt entry

### The composer

`PromptComposer` is a `TextArea` with attachments, context chips and a send control. On phones it is pinned above the keyboard on a glass bar over the scrolling transcript (NDS-9). On desktop and web it sits at the bottom of the conversation pane.

- Start at one line and grow with the text up to 40% of the pane height, then scroll inside. The composer never pushes the latest message out of view.
- Keep model, tools and mode controls in the composer, beside the send control, with the destination of the next message in words (for example "Private AI"). Settings hold account, data and notification choices, never the model picker.
- Write the placeholder as an example of valid input ("Ask about this folder, or type @ to add a file"). The accessible name comes from the product and stays when the placeholder disappears.
- Accept text, paste and drop. A dragged file highlights the composer as a drop target; an accepted item becomes a `Chip` with a remove button and an upload progress state; a rejected item gets the reason in an `InlineAlert` under the composer ("Only text files can be attached here.").
- Show mentions of files, folders and tools as `Chip` tokens typed with "@", the same model as macOS token fields and M3 input chips.
- Show a character counter as "n/max" in `<tier>-mono` once the text reaches 80% of the limit.
- Disable Send only when the composer is empty. This is the one place where a disabled submit is right.
- Save the draft per thread. Closing a `Sheet` or switching threads keeps unsent text.

| Action | macOS | Windows, Linux, web | iOS, iPadOS | Android |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Send | Return | Enter | Send button (hardware keyboard: Return) | Send button |
| New line | Shift-Return | Shift+Enter | Return key | Enter key |
| Stop generating | Esc or Cmd-. | Esc | Stop button (hardware keyboard: Esc or Cmd-.) | Stop button |

Shortcuts come from the command registry and are written `Mod+Key` (NDS-14). See *Navigation and commands*.

### Starter prompts

- Show three or four starter prompts in an empty thread as suggestion `Chip`s (desktop and web) or `ListRow`s (phones). Write them as real tasks from the product's domain, different enough to show the range: "Summarize what changed in this folder today", "Draft a reply to the last review comment", "Which of my sessions used an external model?".
- Choosing a starter fills the composer and places the caret at the end. The person edits and sends; a starter never sends by itself.
- Remove starters once the thread has a message. They are product copy, single-sourced like every other sentence (NDS-16).

## Streaming responses

The stream is the loading state. Show the first words as soon as they arrive and keep everything else still.

- **Before the first token**, put one progress line in the new `Message`'s body: specific, present tense, from the product ("Searching your notes", "Reading 3 files in docs"). Add a `Spinner` beside it only after 200 ms. Never use "Thinking…", "Processing" or "Loading" alone.
- **Render in chunks** at word or line boundaries so lines do not jitter. Use body type with standard leading; long answers use headings so people can skim and jump.
- **Never steal focus.** Focus stays in the composer or wherever the person left it. The transcript follows new text only while the reader is at the bottom; once they scroll up it stays put, and a floating button jumps back to the newest text.
- **Stop is always one step away.** Send turns into Stop in the same place while a response streams (a symbol swap on Apple platforms, a `duration-fast` cross-fade elsewhere). Esc and Cmd-. stop on Apple platforms, Esc elsewhere, whenever focus is in the composer or transcript (NDS-14). Stop acts at once with no confirmation, keeps everything already shown, marks the message with the status word "Stopped", and offers Retry.
- **Draft while it runs.** The composer stays editable during generation so people can write the next prompt.
- **Collapse the scaffolding.** Reasoning, tool calls and sources sit in `Expander`s, closed by default, with a one-line summary each.
- **Count elapsed time up** for waits over 5 seconds ("12 s"). Show an estimate only when it is reliable.
- **Motion.** No shimmer, typing cursor or pulse under Reduce Motion; text simply appears. Never animate per token. See *Motion*.
- **Assistive technology.** Announce "response started" politely, then the response at paragraph boundaries or on completion, never token by token. Each `Message` is a labelled region with its author.
- **Haptics.** None per token; on phones, optional `success` or `error` when a foreground run ends (see *Feedback and states*).

## Result actions

Every result carries the controls people need to keep or change it. Place them in a row of `IconButton`s under the `Message`, aligned to its text. On desktop the row is always visible on the latest message and appears on hover or focus on older ones; every action is also in the message's context `Menu`. On touch the latest message shows the row and older ones use long-press.

| Action | What it does | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| Copy | Copies the result as rich text, Markdown and plain text | Confirm inline: the glyph swaps to a check and the product's word is announced. No toast. |
| Edit | Reopens the prompt in place for editing and resending | The earlier exchange stays reachable as a version ("1 of 2"). |
| Retry | Generates the result again | Keep earlier results as versions with a version switcher; most likely first. |
| Adjust | Applies a named change: shorter, longer, simpler, more formal, or free text | Guided options first, free text second. Label the new version with the adjustment applied, as a `Tag`. |
| Use | Applies the result somewhere else | Name the target in the label ("Insert in note"). This is the only primary button in the row. |
| Undo | Reverses something a result changed | Name the action ("Undo Insert summary"), offer it in a `Toast`, and bind Mod+Z. |

- Acknowledge every correction. When an adjustment takes effect, the new version says what changed, so people learn what the control does.
- Offer alternate versions when results differ meaningfully. Keep them few enough to fit on one screen, describe how they differ, and put the most likely one first.

## Proof and attestation

NEAR AI's stance is proof rather than promises. The interface shows the proof state of each AI call as a word with a `ProofTag`, and lets anyone look at the evidence. It never describes a call as private, secure, confidential or protected.

| Proof state | Word (Trace core) | Tone | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Verified | Verified | `positive` | The attestation was checked and matched. The only state that is proof. |
| Pending | Pending | `caution` | The check has not finished. Resolves in place. |
| Gateway only | Gateway only | `caution` | Only the gateway's part was attested; the model's own proof is absent. |
| Failed | Check failed | `critical` with a 1px outline | The check ran and did not match. Kept apart from every other state. |
| External | External | `critical` | The call went to an outside model. |
| No proof | No proof | `neutral` | The call was answered with no attestation. A fact, not a failure. |
| Not recorded | Not recorded, Unavailable | `neutral` | The record is missing or unreadable, or the label is one this build does not know. |

- Only Verified is proof. Never draw Pending, Gateway only or Not recorded as verified, and never map an unrecognized label to its nearest neighbor; it reads as Not recorded.
- The word carries the meaning. The tone dot is supplementary, and Check failed adds an outline so it never depends on color (NDS-6, NDS-16).
- Put the `ProofTag` in the same place every time: the `Message` footer, each ledger row, each model row.
- Make the `ProofTag` a button. It opens a `Popover` on desktop and web, or a `Sheet` on phones, with a `KeyValueList` (destination, model, time, proof word) above the raw evidence in a `CodeBlock` with Copy. The summary is in words; the evidence is there for anyone who wants to check it themselves.
- When Pending resolves, update the tag in place without moving the layout, and announce the new word once.

### The Private AI ledger

Trace's Private AI tab is the reference ledger for any product that routes AI calls. Top to bottom:

1. Notices and prompts.
2. Fact rows (status, credential, connected tools), each a `StatusLabel`.
3. Totals as three `StatCard`s: Calls, Verified, Priced. Priced is what calls were priced at, never what was billed.
4. A models card with one row per model and its `ProofTag`.
5. A calls `DataTable`: time in `<tier>-mono` with tabular digits, tool, model, proof, newest first, virtualized.
6. The account card with balance and "Manage billing".

Unknown totals read "—". New calls never shift the row under the pointer; a line above the table counts them ("3 new calls") and inserts them on request.

### Route disclosure

- Wherever a call can be answered in more than one place, show where each one was answered, in words, at the call and in Settings: on this device, Private AI, or the external model's name.
- Give Settings a section for it (Trace: Settings > Private AI) listing which tools send their calls here, which credential answers them, and whether the local record is read. Changes apply at once and say so ("Changes here apply straight away.").
- Name the destination, never the mechanism. On these surfaces do not use proxy, route, routing, endpoint, localhost or backend, and never use private, secure, encrypted, anonymous or protected as promises. A tool "sends its calls here" and this computer "answers" them. "Private AI" is a destination's name and appears only as that name.
- Treat a change of destination as a consent change. See *Consent before data leaves the device*.

| Avoid | Write |
|---|---|
| "Your chats are private and secure." | "Answered by Private AI. Verified." |
| "Processing securely…" | "Checking the proof for this answer" |
| "Anonymous data helps us improve." | "Sends your rating and this response to NEAR AI. Nothing else." |

## Consent before data leaves the device

### The review card

When something is about to leave the device, the decision happens on a review card placed where the person already is (Trace puts it in the inspector, never in a modal). Anatomy, top to bottom:

1. Heading and enrollment `Chip`.
2. What would be sent, with sizes in `<tier>-mono` ("would send 18 KB · on disk 42 KB").
3. A preview of the opening content on a quiet `Card`, at most 8 lines.
4. Redactions as inline `Chip`s with spoken labels, and a `Notice` when something that still looks personal remains.
5. The caveat sentence that concedes what the scrubbing cannot promise.
6. Consent scopes and eligibility.
7. Optional verdict (`RadioGroup` row: Worked / Partly / Failed) and a `TextArea` with an "n/max" counter.
8. The `ConsentBlock`, directly above the action it governs.
9. Actions: the primary action (Trace: Contribute) first, then links: Look inside, Keep, Dismiss. A refusal appears as an `InlineAlert` under the buttons.

- Keep the primary action disabled, with its reason in words beside it, until every precondition holds: the service is answering, the preview is current, the consent words are present, and the item is eligible. A tooltip alone is not enough.
- Never give an irreversible send a keyboard shortcut or the Return key. Return belongs to the safe action (Keep, or Undo after an approval).
- Rebuild the preview after input settles (300 ms) so the card always shows what would really be sent.
- State the whole scope on the armed button's tooltip and accessible description: "Sends this trace. Nothing else."

### Look inside

"Look inside" opens a read-only view titled "Exactly what would be sent": a regular-width `Modal` (780) on desktop, a full-height `Sheet` on phones. Its footer has Close only; approve and decline stay on the card.

- Lead with the gate statement, verbatim: "'Exactly what would be sent' is the exact text that would leave this machine. Pattern-based scrubbing may have missed something in it, and nothing here checks that you looked."
- Follow with a sizes card in mono and the destination in words.
- Switch views with a `SegmentedControl`: the content itself (virtualized, with Copy everything and "Load more" that states what remains), and Search (focused on open and on Mod+F, with a match count).
- Render redaction marks as inline mono `Chip`s on `accent-tint`, each with a spoken label from the product.

### Consent blocks

- Put the product's consent sentence in a `ConsentBlock` on the `consent` ground, verbatim, directly above the button it governs. Adapt only sentence case and platform punctuation; never paraphrase per platform. New consent wording is drafted, approved and only then built.
- Use one block per decision. Link to full terms; never scroll legal text inside the block.

### Checkbox-gated confirmations

- Gate confirmations that grant lasting consent with a `Checkbox`: going public, letting a new reviewer read data, turning on automatic sending. The checkbox row is a full sentence stating the consequence. Confirm is drawn prominent but is never the default and never answers Return; it enables only after the tick; Esc cancels (NDS-15).
- Never add a checkbox that gates nothing. Trace removed its acknowledgement checkbox on Contribute because another path approved the same item without it, and moved the sentence the checkbox carried beside the button. If a gate can be bypassed, replace it with honest copy.

### Trust relaxes what may be sent, never what may be said

- Offer automatic behavior as an explicit mode per scope (Trace: Ask me / Automatic / Never, one name on every surface). Automatic is a grant of consent: set it only through a confirmation, never silently. Never is absolute.
- Automatic changes whether each item waits for the person. It never changes the disclosures, the ledger, the proof words or the ability to look inside.
- When anything the consent depended on changes (the destination, the account or device, the reviewer, the allowed uses), stop the automatic mode and say so in a `Notice` that names what changed ("Automatic contributing stopped"), with a button that turns it back on and states that doing so agrees to the new settings.
- Ask for access at first use, never at launch. A pre-permission screen has one Continue button, no incentives, and nothing that imitates the system's own prompt.

## Agents acting on the user's behalf

### Tool calls and approvals

A `ToolCall` sits inline in the transcript: a status glyph, a verb-and-object summary ("Read 3 files in docs"), a status word, and an `Expander` with inputs and outputs in a `CodeBlock`. It is collapsed by default and opens on failure.

| What the agent wants to do | Example | Approval |
|---|---|---|
| Read inside a scope already granted | Read files in the open project | Runs; shown as a `ToolCall` |
| Make a reversible change | Rename files, edit a draft | Allow once / Always allow / Deny; checkpoint first |
| Send to people or off the device | Send an email, post a comment, upload | Allow once / Deny only; shows exactly what will be sent |
| Destroy or overwrite without history | Delete files, empty a folder | Never automated; the agent prepares, the person acts |
| Spend money or commit | Purchase, subscribe, book | Never automated; the agent fills the cart or draft, the person completes it |

- Show approvals inline as a `ToolCall` in its waiting state, with the action in words and the exact target. Use a `Modal` only when the step is consequential and irreversible. Queue approvals; never stack them.
- Order the buttons Deny, Always allow, Allow once, with Allow once trailing as the primary (NDS-19). Esc denies. Return answers Allow once only for reversible changes; for sends it is prominent with no Return key.
- Name the scope of Always allow beside it ("Always allow renaming files in this project"), list every standing grant in Settings with Revoke, and never offer Always allow for sends, deletions or purchases.
- While an approval waits, the run reads "Waiting for you" with a `caution` dot and elapsed time counting up.
- When a step is denied, the transcript says what was skipped ("Skipped: send email") and the run continues without it or stops.

### Checkpoints and undo

- Create a checkpoint before a run's first change, and keep a change list as the run proceeds, with per-item revert.
- Name every undo ("Undo Rename 12 files"). Offer it in a `Toast`, in the Edit menu on desktop, and on Mod+Z.
- Offer "Revert all changes from this run" in the `AgentRun` header menu. After a revert, scroll to and highlight what was restored.
- Say before approval when a step cannot be undone ("This can't be undone after it's sent.").

### Long runs

`AgentRun` holds a run's title (the task in words), a `StatusLabel` (Running, Waiting for you, Paused, Stopped, Done, Failed), elapsed time counting up, the steps, the current step's text, Pause and Stop, the approval queue, and at the end a summary with the change list.

- Show "Step 3 of 7" with a determinate `ProgressBar` or `StepProgress` once the plan is known; before that, an indeterminate `ProgressBar` with an activity log. Never fake an even pace.
- Pause keeps state. Stop ends the run and keeps completed steps and the checkpoint. Confirm Stop only when it discards work that cannot be resumed.
- Keep runs going when people leave, and report on the system surface each platform provides:

| Platform | While running | Needs approval | Finished |
|---|---|---|---|
| iOS, iPadOS | Live Activity: 8 hours at most, one control (Stop), updated only on change, redacted on the Lock Screen | Notification: Time Sensitive only when the approval expires within the hour, otherwise Active | Active notification; the Live Activity ends at once and lingers 15 to 30 minutes with the summary |
| macOS | Menu bar item showing the running state | Notification (Active or Time Sensitive) | Active notification |
| Android | Ongoing progress notification | High-importance channel | Default-importance notification |
| Windows, Linux | Taskbar or tray progress | Toast notification | Toast notification |
| Web | The run's status word in the tab title | Web Push only if granted | Web Push only if granted |

- Give notifications at most two actions, Review and Not now. No notification action may approve a send, a deletion or a purchase.
- Never put prompt or output text in a notification, Live Activity or widget; these surfaces are visible to others. Use a hidden-preview line such as "Agent needs your approval".
- Never use Critical. A digest of finished runs is Passive. See *Feedback and states* for the full notification rules.

## Confidence as reasons

- Express confidence as the reason behind a result: "Matches 3 of your notes from last week", "Because this folder is set to Ask me". Keep attribution factual, never flattering.
- Use percentages only where people expect statistics. A result never says "97% match".
- Change the presentation by threshold. High confidence: act or offer as the default. Middle: offer with the reason and ask to confirm. Low: show nothing, especially for suggestions nobody asked for.
- Show confidence only after checking that it tracks quality.
- Cite sources as links under the result, collapsed by default, so people can check claims that matter.

## Feedback on results

- Put thumbs-up and thumbs-down `IconButton`s in the result action row. They are toggles (pressed state) with accessible names from the product. Feedback is always voluntary and never interrupts.
- After a thumbs-down, offer optional reasons as `Chip`s whose labels state the consequence: "Wrong facts", "Too long", "Don't suggest this tool here". Act on the choice at once, persist it, and confirm inline ("You'll see fewer suggestions like this.").
- Say what feedback sends before it is sent: "Sends your rating and this response to NEAR AI." Include more of the conversation only when the person ticks an unticked `Checkbox`.
- Keep implicit signals (copies, retries) on the device unless the person opted in.

## Errors and refusals

- Write errors as problem, cause, solution, in a neutral tone, with no "Error:" prefix and no blame (see *Feedback and states*).
- Show a failed response inside its `Message` as an `InlineAlert` line in `critical-text`. Keep whatever arrived before the failure, and offer Retry, Edit prompt or a different model.
- Write a refusal as what cannot be done, why when that is safe to say, and how to get a useful result, with an example request. Never pretend to comply and never lecture.
- State when a limit lifts only when the time is known ("Available again at 14:30"). Otherwise say what still works.
- When a model is unavailable or the device is offline, name what is unavailable and the fallback (an on-device model, or the manual path).
- Show a failed tool call expanded inside the run; the run continues if it can, and its summary lists every failure.
- Keep technical detail in an `Expander` with a `CodeBlock` and Copy.

| Situation | Write |
|---|---|
| Model timed out | "The answer stopped before it finished because the model took too long. Try again, or switch to the on-device model." |
| Request refused | "Unable to use that request. Try describing the change you want, for example 'Summarize the open review comments'." |
| Tool failed in a run | "Couldn't rename 2 of 14 files because they are open in another app. Close them and choose Retry." |

## Privacy

- Process on the device first. When a task needs a server, say where it will go before it goes, and let people see what is sent.
- Send only the context a task needs, shown as removable `Chip`s.
- Ask before using personal data to train or improve models: a separate opt-in, off by default, never bundled with the consent a feature needs, revocable at any time, with retention stated. Label the choice by the use people will recognize.
- Treat ambient surfaces as public. Lock Screen, notifications, widgets, menu-bar peeks and the edge rail never show prompts or outputs.
- Let people delete a conversation with Undo, and view and delete remembered items one at a time.
- Keep credentials and API keys in the Keychain or Keystore. Secure fields never echo a key back.
- Collect consent without dark patterns: nothing pre-ticked, no incentives, and declining is as easy to find as accepting.

## App Intents, Shortcuts and Snippets

- Define each agent action once in the command registry (verb, object, parameters, whether it needs confirmation or authentication) and render it per platform: App Intents and App Shortcuts on Apple platforms, app shortcuts and App Actions on Android, jump lists on Windows, the `CommandPalette` and deep links on web (NDS-14).
- Offer at most 10 App Shortcuts, each with at most one optional parameter whose values are predictable. When input is missing, default to the likeliest value and offer a short list.
- Confirm consequential actions with a confirmation snippet: Cancel on the left, a specific verb on the right ("Send", "Start run"), at most 400 pt tall. The view carries the meaning and never repeats the spoken dialogue. Report results with a result snippet and Done, deep-linking for detail.
- A control that starts an agent task animates until the task is done, redacts when the device is locked, and requires unlocking for anything consequential.
- Never present the agent as Siri or as a system assistant. Spoken responses are short, stand alone without the screen, use no humor and omit the app name.

## Do and don't

- Do show the proof word and let people open the evidence. Don't call anything private, secure or protected in copy.
- Do show exactly what would be sent, above the button that sends it. Don't put the consent sentence in a tooltip.
- Do keep Stop in the same place as Send. Don't move focus or scroll while output streams.
- Do let agents prepare destructive actions and purchases for the person to complete. Don't automate them.
